Auditing demand response, thermal service and equity

kpi-families-audit-v1 and demand-response-audit-v1 check thermal service, equity and demand-response accounting. Available from v1.9.8, they check model consistency without changing dispatch, settlement or rewards. They do not calibrate the model against real equipment.

Interfaces and evidence

env.get_kpi_family_audit() returns member/district thermal populations and district equity coverage. It accepts the same optional comfort_band, control_condition and baseline_condition as evaluation. It is read-only. V2 adds building_thermal_service_audit_*, district_thermal_service_audit_* and district_equity_audit_*. The default scorecard pairs service rates with coverage. The export manifest identifies definitions, DR preparation/aggregation and the resolved reward class, configured attributes and available public parameters. Custom reward semantics still require inspection. Core settlement does not automatically add DR cash to the grid bill or reward.

Thermal/equity evaluation supports flat and entity interfaces. Demand response continues to require entity. Simultaneous overlapping DR requests remain explicitly unsupported; the existing rejection is preserved. No UI application is modified.

Demand response

The baseline is the mean of the completed pre-event net-power samples. The preparation interval count is ceil(baseline_window_seconds / step_seconds), with at least one interval, so the requested duration is never rounded down. A 3600 s window needs four 900 s samples or two 2500 s samples. The effective duration is reported. This does not resample a dataset or create history before an episode.

The preparation period and settled event interval must have stable structural topology. A member/asset change makes the reference invalid, including after an initially valid cached baseline. Invalid intervals retain requested energy but receive no inferred delivery, revenue or penalty. They are excluded from valid service denominators and expose a reason: insufficient history, nonfinite history, no members or topology change. A lower penalty due to missing reference is not evidence of better control. A rolling baseline remains an operational reference, not a counterfactual causal model; previous load/DR behaviour can affect it.

For step duration h, baseline power B, actual power P, target T and tolerance epsilon, in the declared load perspective:

  • up: delivered power is P - B; down: B - P.

  • Signed delivered energy is delivered power times h.

  • Credited energy is min(max(delivered_power, 0), T) * h.

  • Shortfall is max(T - delivered_power - epsilon, 0) * h.

  • Revenue and penalty multiply their corresponding energy by the declared prices; net revenue is revenue minus penalty. Compliance is credited / valid requested energy. No valid requested energy gives an undefined rate.

Member signed delivery, requested energy and cash are additive. Member credit is the uncapped positive contribution; member shortfall uses an equal share of the target and does not apply district tolerance. They are allocation diagnostics, not additive versions of the district credited energy or shortfall. For example, two members delivering +2 and -3 kW against a 2 kW downward request over 15 minutes give district delivery -0.25 kWh, credit 0, shortfall 0.75 kWh. Individual credits sum to 0.5 kWh and individual shortfalls to 1 kWh; cash still reconciles. These established allocation rules are unchanged.

Request IDs must be unique. An optional request-file purpose column or demand_response.request_purposes map declares service, stress or unspecified. The map takes precedence and unknown IDs/labels are rejected. Without a declaration the purpose is unspecified. Feasibility, purpose and observed fulfilment are separate questions; a failed request is never automatically called stress. This audit does not prove joint DR scheduling feasibility.

District demand_response_audit_{all|service|stress|unspecified}_* metrics expose unique events, active/valid interval counts, exclusion reasons, requested/valid requested energy, signed delivery, credit, shortfall and cash. Baseline coverage is valid / active intervals; energy coverage is valid requested / requested energy. Empty denominators are undefined, not an automatic success.

Comfort and resilience

The evaluation population is executed intervals while a member is active. An unexecuted current observation is excluded even during a partial episode. Inactive gaps never become fictitious zero-temperature samples. An occupied interval has finite occupancy greater than zero. Temperature, both setpoints and band must be finite, band nonnegative and heating setpoint no greater than cooling. Unknown occupancy and unevaluable occupied samples have explicit counts/coverage.

Cold means temperature < heating_setpoint - band; hot means temperature > cooling_setpoint + band. Equality is comfortable. Rates divide discomfort intervals by evaluated occupied intervals, not person count. Native per-interval building bands are honoured unless an explicit evaluation override is supplied. The legacy rolling ratios now use cumulative observed occupancy; cold/hot deviation min/max/mean condition on actual occupied violations. No violation gives undefined severity, not a fabricated zero deviation.

The audit exposes occupied/evaluated/discomfort hours and cold/hot degree-hours. Degree-hours use absolute deviation from the corresponding setpoint, restricted to violating intervals. During outages, the same definitions apply to the occupied outage population. No evaluated occupied outage sample gives an undefined outage rate. District audit rates pool exposure and energy before division; historical district comfort aggregates retain their member-mean form.

Service energy covers space cooling, space heating, hot water and base electricity. It sums demand energy across those services; it is not primary electrical acquisition. Device/demand series are converted from dataset resolution to control-step kWh; thermal storage balances already use control-step kWh. Deficit is calculated separately for each service before summing, so oversupply of one cannot cancel an unmet demand elsewhere. The legacy unserved-energy caller also uses these corrected units/component caps. This is reporting, not a dispatch fix.

unserved_service_ratio is total deficit / total known expected service energy; the outage variant restricts both to known outage intervals. Missing/negative energy inputs exclude that interval and reduce energy coverage. No expected energy gives undefined audit ratios. The historical low-level zero-demand unserved function still returns zero for compatibility; use the audit’s population and coverage to distinguish zero, unknown and not applicable. Appliances and EV departure service retain their dedicated audits from iterations 1 and 2.

Equity

Relative benefit remains 100 * (reference_cost - actual_cost) / reference_cost. Finite costs and strictly positive reference cost are required. The audit reports eligible population, excluded unknown/nonpositive-reference counts and coverage. Costs use each member’s executed active participation and the actual evaluation conditions, which appear in the V2 attributes. Different participation periods remain different exposures; this is relative bill benefit, not equal opportunity.

Losers include eligible negative benefits. Gini and top-20 concentration use the nonnegative part, retaining zero positions in the eligible population. Top-20 uses ceil(0.2 * eligible_members). No positive benefit mass makes both undefined. The audit independently uses a sorted-rank Gini identity and is checked against the legacy pairwise calculation and hand examples. Read concentration with losers and coverage. BPR retains manually declared rich/poor groups and positive-part group means; absent/invalid groups or a nonpositive rich mean are undefined.

Validation and bounded real runs

python scripts/audit/audit_kpi_families.py \
  --schema data/datasets/pt_rec_100/evaluation/rec_100_allin.json \
  --request-id development_dr_1 --policy normal --seed 0 \
  --output /tmp/dr-audit.json

The CLI includes the required preparation and terminal observation. It independently reconstructs DR validity, energy/cash and additive allocations from completed raw power samples; it compares exported district DR totals. Thermal/equity evidence is a snapshot of the separately tested audit populations, not a second physical model. Tolerance is 1e-5 + 1e-7 * max(abs(actual), abs(expected)) in the compared units. An invalid reference can pass accounting consistency while its evaluation quality remains incomplete; the report keeps these statuses separate.

Regression tests cover independent examples, zero/unknown denominators, negative benefits, partial/inactive participation, native bands/overrides, 60/900/3600 s units, already-scaled thermal tanks, duplicate IDs, invalidated cached references, purpose groups, read-only queries and reward preservation. The eight Premium development requests are exercised without editing inputs. The 2023 fourth request coincides with retirement of Member_093’s PV at index 30696: all its 16 intervals have an invalid structural reference. The other seven have complete references. The inputs declare no purpose, so all remain unspecified.

These corrections require rerunning affected comfort, resilience and DR analyses. They do not claim annual validation, equal controller service, global optimality, an empirical thermal model or calibration. Full temporal/data alignment and reproducibility remain iteration 6; long-run resource validation remains iteration 7.
