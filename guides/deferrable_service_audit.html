Deferrable appliance service audit

deferrable-service-audit-v1 accounts for executed appliance service independently of the full profiles reserved by start_cycle. Existing deferrable_appliance_service_* KPIs retain their historical definitions; use the new building_deferrable_appliance_audit_* and district_deferrable_appliance_audit_* families for complete request accounting. This definition is released in version 1.9.5.

Population and states

A logical request is identified by member, appliance name and cycle ID. It is offered when its admissible start window intersects an actually executed interval while the appliance is active, and at least one full profile can fit before the episode’s terminal observation and the request deadline. Eligibility is a calendar/window definition, not a claim that electrical supply was sufficient. A request blocked by an outage can therefore become missed service.

Each offered request has exactly one state:

Removal reasons are asset_removed and member_removed. A start deadline already elapsed takes precedence over a later removal. A physically completed request remains completed. Pending/running requests at an intermediate query are unresolved; they are not counted as completed or converted to failure just because a report was requested. Completion uses an absolute/relative energy tolerance of 1e-6 * max(1, requested_kWh); residuals remain visible rather than being rounded away.

The audit covers loaded catalogues of members active at least once in the episode; never-activated members are outside this population. Excluded catalogue requests identify expired_before_episode, expired_before_activation, not_yet_available, removed_before_available, outside_episode_service_horizon, or already_accounted_previous_instance. Excluded energy is reported separately and does not enter the service denominator. The terminal observation is never a served interval. A fresh episode has no offered requests until its first interval executes.

Reinstallation/re-entry retains the removed instance’s history and does not offer an already offered logical request again. Requests not yet available before removal may be offered by the later instance. Member IDs are part of the identity: moving an appliance to a different member creates a different request population. Aggregated exports contain one row per logical request, preferring its offered owner to excluded copies. duplicate_offered_count and physical residuals expose unexpected duplicate ownership instead of concealing it.

Energy and ratios

Only actual per-interval device consumption is attributed to running profiles. Future reservations do not count as service. Cancellation clears future reserved energy while preserving the executed prefix. Across offered requests:

requested_energy_kwh = served_energy_kwh + final_unserved_energy_kwh + outstanding_energy_kwh

physical_balance_residual_kwh = physical_energy_kwh - served_energy_kwh

service_balance_residual_kwh is the residual of the first equation. Both should be zero within floating point tolerance. The independent physical total includes all historical appliance instances; unattributed consumption remains visible. The interval ledger runs in both normal steps and internal no-feedback rollouts. Queries are pure, repeated interval recording is idempotent, and reset clears it.

Every count, exclusion reason and state reason is exported. Zero denominators are undefined (None/empty CSV cells), not perfect service. Interpret completion ratios together with coverage and energy; a running cycle has delivered some service even though it has not completed.

API and export

env.get_deferrable_service_audit() returns the definition, per-request records, building summaries and district summary. Request times use global dataset indexes, including cancellation in a simulation starting partway through the calendar.

env.export_final_kpis() writes an adjacent .deferrable_service.json alongside its CSV or Parquet KPIs and .manifest.json. The manifest identifies the audit definition and denominators. Multi-community exports provide this evidence for each child community; no cross-community logical-request deduplication is claimed.

Action/observation interfaces, datasets, price conventions and physical dispatch rules are unchanged. Sparse calendars are no longer filtered by a local loading window or the first membership period. This restores requests previously omitted from partial-window and re-entry experiments and can change their consumption. Lifecycle fixes affect appliance removal/re-entry: interrupted requests stay cancelled and cannot be replayed as a new request under the same identity. No empirical calibration of appliance profiles, user satisfaction model, annual controller ranking or general repair of other assets’ member re-entry accounting is claimed.
